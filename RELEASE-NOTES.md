## Going Public Server 1.0.17.1

New since 1.0.17 (6 November). **Please update the server and the Workshop mod together.** A player with 1.0.17 can still join a 1.0.17.1 server and gets a note in the chat - but without the error-report question and without the original under translated lines. With the AMP setting "Only the same mod version" the server refuses 1.0.17 and names the version needed.

### Chat and error reports
- **Chat translation:** the server translates the chat between German and English (AMP switch "Chat translation", Server tab, on by default). Each player reads the others' lines in the language of their game, the original small and grey below; your own lines stay as you wrote them, "lol", "gg" and links stay as they are. It runs on the server itself - no account, no service on the internet, the chat never leaves the server. The first start sets it up once in `bigambitions/bamp-translate` (about 550 MB, outside the game folder, so not in AMP's backups); the game starts at once and the translation follows within minutes. Each player can switch it off under Options, tab Mods ("Translate the chat"). Console command `translate <text>` for a test.
- **Error reports to the server:** on servers that collect them (AMP switch "Player error reports", Server tab, on by default) the game asks each player once whether it may send its errors, warnings and the mod's check lines; it can be changed under Options, tab Mods. Then they go to the server every 30 seconds while playing - the game takes out Windows user names, IP addresses, Steam IDs and keys first. After a crash, or with the console command `playerlog <player>`, the whole Player.log comes too. The server keeps everything in `bigambitions/player-reports` (`summary.json` with every kind of error: how often, who, which build and system), writes one line to the console every 10 minutes, the command `reports` lists the most frequent errors, and after 14 days it deletes everything. None of it is public, and nothing goes to anybody else.

### Going Online 0.7.1: the cable provider in an office
- **One office for everything (Going Online 0.7):** the cable provider is now founded in an office - the headend, the customer center and all workplaces under one roof; it needs no warehouse any more, and a separate "Cable customer center" is no longer offered (an existing one keeps working). A headend that still stands in a warehouse moves into the office by itself once a "Cable provider" is founded there (or the customer center's office is taken) and a CMTS stands in it; the warehouse becomes an ordinary warehouse again, its devices still count.
- **Hiring by workplaces:** the recruitment agencies offer the cable provider only the jobs that have a workplace in the office; if one is missing, a message says which. The app's Staff tab shows whom the agencies offer right now.
- **Set up like a real office:** the customer office now has the requirements of the game's office businesses - work computers, toilets (one per 100 m²) and sinks (one per 200 m²) - and a CMTS; BizMan lists them under Insight and makes to-do tasks of them, the app says what is still missing. As in the game, the staff work only when everything stands and every computer workplace has a desk and a chair. Like every office business, founding the cable provider needs the Manhattan Business School's course "Office Businesses" (since the move into offices it asked for the headquarters' course by mistake).
- **The cable provider in BizMan as a business of its own:** it is listed under "Businesses & real estate" - with its connections ("Cable provider (91 connections)") and Going Online's satisfaction - instead of under "Warehouses". Its page opens on Insight and has the tabs Deliveries, Fleet and Inventory as well.
- **A fleet instead of delivery drivers:** the Fleet tab shows every vehicle of the headend - your own (parked within 80 m) and the leased ones - and who drives it right now. Under "Assigned to" a vehicle can get a person of its own (cable technicians and switching staff only a van); otherwise Going Online hands out the vehicles each shift by itself. Before, the warehouse's driver slots with delivery drivers and empty places stood there.
- **Wholesalers deliver the devices:** a wholesaler's delivery contract now offers the cable provider. BizMan's Deliveries tab lists the devices; they come at 8 o'clock onto the storage shelves in the office (pallet shelves count too).
- **Devices at every wholesaler:** cable modems, ONTs, IPTV boxes and smartcards stand on the shelves of **every wholesaler** (five shelves per store, every good keeps shelves of its own); the *Devices* page names the wholesaler nearest to the headend. Importers with electronics deliver them later like any import (headquarters, purchasing agent, warehouse); devices in your warehouses count as stock. Before, only two wholesalers picked at random had the devices, and on no shelf at that.
- **The cable provider in the map filter:** the map's list of businesses now has "Cable provider" (and "Cable customer center" where an old one exists); the button next to it moves the map to your own building.
- **Network masters at the headquarters:** network masters can now be hired for the headquarters too (City Workforce Inc.); they work at its computers, get a shift there and count in Going Online like those in the headend.
- **The cable jobs at the recruitment agencies:** when the cable provider is picked at an agency, the mod checks right then that the agency has the cable jobs and that the game knows the cable provider's jobs, adds what is missing and logs what it found. It also tops the agencies up every 20 seconds, and a failure while setting up the business types no longer stops the other steps. Before, the agency answered "offers no candidates with this skill type" for the cable provider.
- **A real business logo for the cable provider:** in a warehouse the game gave it only the black warehouse logo, and BizMan showed no logo editor. Now it gets a logo of its own once (shapes like electronics stores and web agencies), and BizMan's Settings show the game's logo editor (shape, colours, font, your own icons).
- **Going Online with its own models:** all seven pieces of headend equipment (CMTS 3.0 and 3.1, OLT, Sat, CAS, Voice, IPTV) are now real 19-inch racks with devices, cables and blinking LEDs, each in its own colour with a name plate. Modems, ONT, set-top box and TV card have their own models, every device new icons in the game's style.
- **Service vans in the city traffic:** while Going Online technicians are out, up to three white high-roof service vans with your business logo, a ladder on the roof and a beacon drive in the city's traffic.
- **Going Online's devices on the shelves:** modems, ONTs, set-top boxes and TV cards look for their model where the game keeps goods' models - the server no longer writes "no model for ba:itemname_smartphone1" 2.5 times a second into the console.
- **Prices in Going Online can be typed again:** the text fields (tariff prices, device prices, street search) and the radio page's search now sit on the game's UI layer. Before, the digits and letters typed also fired the game's shortcuts and the field lost its focus.
- **The Going Online page uses the full width** of the phone menu like the game's apps. Where a row still does not fit (buildings with fiber, tariffs on narrow screens), its buttons go onto a second line instead of being cut off on the right.
- Going Online on its own (single player, Steam Workshop) is now version **0.7.1** as well.

### Traffic and driving
- **Pedestrians wait properly at traffic lights:** at crosswalks with traffic lights, pedestrians now start only when the cars' red lasts long enough to get all the way across - otherwise they wait for the next red. Whoever is already on the crosswalk when the light is about to change hurries. Cars drive on green as before; until now people often still walked across when the cars already had green.
- **Cars stop for pedestrians:** with someone on a crosswalk, cars stop before it - turning cars and cars that just got green too - and drive on once it is clear. Where the host's cars drive near other players, the pedestrians look out for coming cars themselves and only then go.
- **Faster on the open road:** on bridges, and where no traffic light, junction or turn comes for 150 m, self-driving goes up to 60 km/h instead of 40 (where the lane has no speed of its own) and slows back to 40 smoothly before the next light, junction or bend. From that speed it brakes in time for cars and people.
- **Fast-forward time in the car:** the window opens above the vehicle panel instead of on top of it.
- **Nothing over the driving key hints any more:** while the game shows its key hints at the bottom left (driving, placing furniture), the version line and the ⏩ button stand to their right instead of over "Handbrake", "Horn" and "Indicators".
- **No more stranding beside the pavement:** when a car stops where the walkable area is missing (at the pier without a free parking spot, for one), the mod puts the character on the nearest walkable point after getting out. Before, it turned on the spot and floated over the water. The same after a seat repair.

### Other
- **Server crash fixed:** shortly after a player came into the world the server could crash ("Got a UNKNOWN while executing native code") when it read a kind of message for the first time. It now prepares every kind of message at start; so does the players' game.
- **Shop signs (F7) with the business logo:** the first entry of the picture list is now "★ The game's business logo". Apply puts the logo from BizMan back on the chosen sign type; the other signs keep their picture. Your own pictures and the game's logo stand side by side.

### For server admins (AMP)
- **Update:** press *Update* in AMP, then restart the instance. AMP shows the new switches "Player error reports" and "Chat translation" (Server tab, both on by default) after that restart - it reads the template only when the instance starts.
- **The first start** sets up the chat translator in the background (about 400 MB download, then 550 MB in `bigambitions/bamp-translate`, outside the game folder and so not in AMP's backups; about 400 MB of memory while the server runs). The game does not wait for it; the console shows how far it is (`[BAMP-Start] Chat-Übersetzung: ...`).
- **New console commands:** `reports` (the players' most frequent errors), `playerlog <player>` (asks that player's game for its whole Player.log), `translate <text>` (a test of the translator).
